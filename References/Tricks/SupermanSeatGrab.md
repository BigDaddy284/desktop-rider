# SupermanSeatGrab

**Kind:** Freestyle trick

## What it is

A superman where one hand lets go of the bar and grabs the bike further back, so the body can stretch out much further behind. Real freestyle bikes have grab holes cut in the side panels under the seat for this.

## The real move: key poses in order

| # | When | Rider | Bike |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lip to 15% | Neutral, standing. One hand already loosening. | Nose up at the ramp angle. |
| 2 | 15% to 30% | Feet leave the pegs and go back as in a superman. As the body passes over the seat, one hand moves from the grip to the grab hole or the seat edge on the same side. | The front rises slightly as the weight moves back. |
| 3 | 30% to 60% (the peak) | Body fully stretched behind the bike, one arm straight back to the seat, the other straight forward to the bar. Chest turned a little toward the grabbing arm. Legs straight and together. | Level. With only one hand on the bars, the bars stay straight because the rider keeps that arm relaxed. |
| 4 | 60% to 85% | Pulls on the seat hand to bring the hips back over the seat, then the hand goes back to the grip. Knees forward, feet to the pegs. | Nose drops for the landing. |
| 5 | Landing | Standing, both hands firmly on, throttle on. | Rear slightly first, deep compression. |

The body ends up further back and lower than in a plain superman: the shoulders are over the rear mudguard, not over the seat. The rider's weight hanging off the back makes the front noticeably light.

## Version 1, for comparison

**Timing and bike motion**

- **Stage:** steel freestyle ramp, 38° at the lip, lip 0.5 m high, landing on a dirt mound 0.8 m high with a 31° down slope. Bike speed 3 m/s. Gravity is scaled to 2.6 m/s² so a small ramp gives real-looking hang time.
- **Timeline (seconds):** pulls away 0 to 0.6, leaves the lip at 1.46, in the air for 1.79, lands at 3.25, brakes from 4.04, stopped at 5.08. That is 122 keyed frames at 24 a second, 62 sprite frames at 12 a second.
- **Height:** the centre of bike and rider peaks about 2.4 m above the ground.
- **Bike in the air:** 38° nose up at the lip, held for the first 0.27 s, then eases over to 27° nose down just before landing. No roll, no yaw, bars straight. Rear wheel spins 15% faster than on the ground, front wheel slows to half. Suspension hangs out in the air (30 mm rear, 45 mm fork), compresses on the ramp face and on landing, with one small overshoot after the hit.
- **Rider on the ground:** seated to pull away, up into the attack position by 0.7 s, legs soak the ramp, a small pop at the lip, legs soak the landing, sits back to rest while braking.
- **Trick timing after take-off:** starts moving at 0.25 s, fully in the pose at 0.68 s, held until 1.07 s, back in the riding position by 1.50 s. That leaves 0.29 s on the bike before the wheels touch.
- **Pose in the code:** hips 0.41 m back and 0.29 m up, body flat and twisted 34° toward the camera, feet 1.9 m back, the hand nearest the camera on the seat 0.4 m behind centre, the far hand on its grip.

**Pose in the version 1 sprite sheet**

Body flat and long behind the bike, further back than Superman, with the near arm reaching down to the seat and the far arm forward to the bar.

## What to change for the 3D version

Good match. The owner's bike is a KTM 50 with no grab holes, so the hand holds the seat edge; keep that, do not invent holes. In 3D the grabbing hand should be chosen by which side the camera is not hiding, or always the same hand so it looks consistent from every angle. Add the nose-up reaction as for Superman.
