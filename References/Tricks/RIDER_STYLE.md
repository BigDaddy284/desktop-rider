# The rider's own style

What the owner's photographs and videos of the rider show about how this particular rider rides. Where this differs from the general technique in the other sheets, **this file wins**, because the aim is for the 3D rider to move like the real one.

Source: a set of 59 action photographs and 2 phone videos supplied by the owner on 13 November 2026, all of the same young rider on the same KTM 50 SX. They are kept in the project files, not in this public repository. The photographs are professional race photographs and the photographer's copyright is assumed, so they must not be reproduced.

**Which photographs to trust for what.** Only 3 photographs show the correct kit (sky blue, pink and black, with the Fox helmet). The other 56 show older kits (a white hoodie with purple and yellow trousers, or black and white) and a bike with older graphics. Use the older ones **for posture, movement and proportions only, never for colours or graphics.** The 2 videos show a different, darker kit and are also for movement only.

## Riding posture

### Cornering (17 photographs, the best-covered move)

This is the rider's most consistent and recognisable pose.

- **Sits right at the front of the seat**, against the back of the tank.
- **Inside leg straight out ahead**, not to the side: the boot is level with or above the front axle, beside the front wheel, toe pointing up. It is held clear of the ground, not dragged.
- **Outside foot on its peg**, outside knee against the bike.
- **Elbows high**, the outside one highest, level with the grips or above.
- **Head turned into the corner**, eyes on the exit, chin over the inside grip.
- **The bike leans more than the body.** The bike is laid over about 30° to 45° in ruts and berms while the upper body stays nearer upright over it.
- Turns both ways equally well; there is no favourite side.

### Straights

- **Mostly seated**, unlike adult riders who stand for most of a lap. This is normal for a small rider on a 50.
- Chest forward and low over the bar pad, head low behind the number plate.
- Elbows out wide and up.
- Stands up on the pegs for jumps, bumps and the face of a climb.

### Jumps (about 14 photographs, from the side, front and behind)

- Small jumps only, with the wheels about a wheel's height off the ground.
- **Stands on the pegs**, knees bent, hips over the pegs, arms bent.
- The bike flies **level or slightly nose up** and stays straight. No whips or tricks appear in any photograph.
- Looks ahead to the landing, not down.

### Starts (video, 13 seconds, filmed from behind at the start gate)

| Time after the gate drops | What happens |
|---|---|
| Waiting | Seated forward, both feet on the ground, **elbows very high and wide**, revving. Shoulders hunched forward. |
| 0 to 0.2 s | Bike launches. A puff of blue two-stroke smoke and a spray of dirt from the rear wheel. |
| 0.2 to 0.6 s | Feet stay down, skimming the ground beside the bike for balance. |
| 0.6 to 1.0 s | Feet come up onto the pegs. Body stays forward and seated. |
| After 1 s | Riding away seated, elbows still up. |

The second video is filmed from far away across the track and only shows the rider as a small figure going through muddy corners. It adds little detail.

## What this changes in the reference sheets

| Sheet | Change |
|---|---|
| RidingLoop | The default riding pose should be **seated forward**, not standing. Corners use the pose above. |
| RideOff | Use the start sequence above: both feet down, elbows high, feet up by about 1 s, blue smoke. |
| Jump | Standing, bike level and straight, small air. The real rider's jumps are lower and flatter than version 1's. |
| Idle | The start-gate video shows the natural resting stance on the bike: seated with both feet on the ground. That supports a feet-down resting pose rather than the version 1 balancing bike. |
| Freestyle tricks | No photograph or video shows the rider doing any trick, whip or scrub. Those sheets stay based on general references. |
